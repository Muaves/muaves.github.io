Council house

A home owned and rented out by a local council (or a housing association) at a below-market rent, for people on low incomes or with particular needs — one form of social housing. Most were built between 1919 and the 1980s; since the 1980 "Right to Buy" scheme, tenants could purchase their council home at a discount, and the overall stock has shrunk.

Bigger problems

  • A long-running housing shortage and a shrinking supply of social/council housing.
  • Rising rents and a cost-of-living squeeze on lower incomes.
  • Growing homelessness and use of temporary accommodation.
  • Pressure on the NHS (waiting lists) and public services.
  • Regional inequality — the North–South divide in jobs and investment.
