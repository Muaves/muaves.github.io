6 · Accents, Regions & Housing

Pronunciation & accents

  • RP (Received Pronunciation) — the "standard", non-regional accent used in broadcasting and often taught to learners.
  • Cockney — East London; dropped "h"s, glottal stops, rhyming slang.
  • Estuary English — South-East England; a softer blend between RP and Cockney.
  • Scouse — Liverpool; distinctive rising intonation.
  • Geordie — Newcastle/North-East; unique vowel sounds and vocabulary.
  • Brummie — Birmingham; flat, nasal tone.
  • Scottish & Welsh — strong national accents, each with many local variants.

UK regions

  • Four nations: England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland.
  • England is split into regions: North East, North West, Yorkshire & the Humber, East Midlands, West Midlands, East of England, London, South East, South West.
  • There's a well-known North–South divide in wealth, house prices and investment.

Council house

A home owned and rented out by a local council (or a housing association) at a below-market rent, for people on low incomes or with particular needs — one form of social housing. Most were built between 1919 and the 1980s; since the 1980 "Right to Buy" scheme, tenants could purchase their council home at a discount, and the overall stock has shrunk.

Bigger problems

  • A long-running housing shortage and a shrinking supply of social/council housing.
  • Rising rents and a cost-of-living squeeze on lower incomes.
  • Growing homelessness and use of temporary accommodation.
  • Pressure on the NHS (waiting lists) and public services.
  • Regional inequality — the North–South divide in jobs and investment.
